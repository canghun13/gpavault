About GPA Vault

GPA Vault is an independent site of free calculators and guides for US high school and college students. It covers the numbers that decide what a student can afford and what they qualify for: GPA, test scores, federal financial aid rules, student loans, and whether a degree pays off.

What the site covers

Each calculator is paired with a guide that explains the rule behind the number, so you can check the result instead of taking it on trust.

  • GPA and grades — GPA, weighted GPA, IB and AP conversions, and what a retake does to your GPA.
  • Federal aid rules — Satisfactory Academic Progress, the Pell Grant lifetime limit, Pell enrollment intensity, and Return of Title IV funds when you withdraw.
  • Student loans — the federal loan limits that changed on July 1, 2026, repayment, and interest capitalization.
  • Costs and outcomes — college cost, degree return on investment, study abroad, internships, and changing majors.

How the numbers are sourced

Figures come from primary sources wherever one exists: the Federal Student Aid Handbook and Dear Colleague Letters from the Department of Education, StudentAid.gov, the College Board, ACT, the IB Organization, IRS publications, and published policies from individual colleges. When schools handle something differently, the guide says so and shows the range instead of presenting one school's policy as the rule.

Federal figures are updated for the current award year. The 2026–27 Pell Grant maximum, the new graduate and Parent PLUS loan limits, and the end of Grad PLUS for new borrowers are reflected across the site. Where a rule is still unsettled, the page says it is unsettled rather than guessing.

The full approach is on the Methodology page, and how pages are written, reviewed and corrected is on the Editorial Policy page.

What GPA Vault is not

GPA Vault is not a lender, a school, or a government agency, and it is not affiliated with the Department of Education or any college. Nothing here is financial, legal or tax advice. The calculators give estimates based on published rules; your financial aid office, loan servicer and StudentAid.gov account have the figures that actually apply to you.

How the site is funded

Every calculator is free and needs no account. Calculations run in your browser and nothing you type is sent to a server. The site is supported by advertising, and advertisers have no say in what the guides recommend. See the Privacy Policy for how ads and analytics use cookies.
