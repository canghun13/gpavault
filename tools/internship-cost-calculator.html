How this is calculated

This adds housing and living costs across the internship's duration, one-time costs like flights or a security deposit, and any tuition fee your school charges for internship course credit — then subtracts any earnings or stipend if the internship is paid. If you enter an estimate for what you could have earned at an alternative summer job, the calculator also shows the fuller opportunity cost: what taking the internship costs you once lost wages are included, not just your direct spending.

The credit-tuition problem people don't expect

Unpaid internships are only legal in most cases if they're tied to a genuine educational experience — and many schools satisfy that requirement by making students enroll in a for-credit internship course, which comes with its own tuition bill, sometimes at the standard per-credit rate. Students at several universities have publicly pushed back on this arrangement in recent years, pointing out that it means working for free while also paying the school. If your program requires this, make sure to include it above — it's frequently the single largest hidden cost of an "unpaid" internship.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do some schools charge tuition for an unpaid internship? Federal labor law generally requires unpaid internships to be tied to a legitimate educational experience, and many schools satisfy this by requiring a for-credit internship course with its own tuition. This means a student can work for free while also paying the school.

Can I get financial aid to cover internship costs? If taken for academic credit, the tuition may be covered by financial aid depending on your enrollment status. Some schools also offer dedicated internship stipends or grants — ask your career center directly.

Is an unpaid internship worth it if I could earn more at a paid summer job? There's no universal answer. NACE research has found students with paid internship experience tend to receive more job offers on average, but outcomes for any specific internship aren't guaranteed.

What if my unpaid internship is in an expensive city? Housing is usually the largest cost. Look into short-term sublets, university-run summer housing, or co-living arrangements, and always ask about housing stipends before assuming none exist.
