Enrollment Intensity

Why Pell math is different from every other kind of federal aid

Since the 2024–25 award year, the Pell Grant no longer uses the old full-time / three-quarter-time / half-time / less-than-half-time categories that every other federal aid program still uses. Instead, Pell prorates per credit hour using enrollment intensity — your credits enrolled divided by your school's full-time threshold, rounded to the nearest whole percent. Two students both classified as "three-quarter-time" under the old system — one with 9 credits and one with 11 — now get meaningfully different Pell amounts (75% vs. 92%), even though every other type of aid they receive still treats them identically.

One credit can be worth hundreds of dollars

Because Pell is now calculated to the exact credit, the jump between credit loads isn't always small. Going from 9 to 10 credits moves your intensity from 75% to 83% — an 8-point jump worth roughly $296 per term at the 2026–27 maximum award, just for one additional credit hour. If you're on the fence about adding a class, it's worth checking what that specific credit is worth in Pell dollars before deciding it isn't worth the extra workload.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Pell Grant enrollment intensity calculated? Credits enrolled divided by your school's full-time threshold (usually 12), rounded to the nearest whole percent, capped at 100%. Your Pell Grant for the term is your scheduled award multiplied by that percentage.

Does every federal aid program use enrollment intensity? No — this is unique to Pell. Every other Title IV program still uses the older full-time/three-quarter/half-time/less-than-half-time categories, so your Pell amount and your loan eligibility can be calculated on completely different scales in the same term.

Can enrollment intensity ever exceed 100%? No. Taking more credits than your school's full-time threshold doesn't increase your per-term Pell award beyond the full scheduled amount.

Can I get Pell Grant for summer classes too? Yes — Pell-eligible students can receive up to 150% of their scheduled award within a single award year, which can open up a summer disbursement on top of fall and spring, as long as you're within your 600% lifetime eligibility limit.

Does my school's full-time credit threshold have to be 12? 12 credits is the federal minimum and what most schools use, but individual schools can set a higher standard. Confirm your school's exact number with your financial aid office.
