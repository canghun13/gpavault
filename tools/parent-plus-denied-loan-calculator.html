Parent PLUS Denied? Extra Student Loan Calculator

When a parent can't get a Parent PLUS Loan, a dependent student can borrow at the higher independent-student limits — $4,000 or $5,000 more a year in Direct Unsubsidized Loans. Check your new limit, what's left under the lifetime cap, and whether your situation actually qualifies.

How much more you can borrow

A dependent undergraduate normally borrows at the dependent limits. If a parent can't obtain a Parent PLUS Loan, the student may borrow at the independent limits instead. The subsidized portion doesn't change; the extra is all unsubsidized.

Who qualifies — and who doesn't

The extra amount is for parents who can't borrow, not parents who won't. Federal Student Aid lists these as qualifying:

  • The parent is denied a PLUS Loan for adverse credit.
  • Other exceptional circumstances the school documents: the parent is incarcerated or their whereabouts are unknown; the parent is in bankruptcy and a court letter says they may not take on more debt; the parent's income is limited to public assistance or disability benefits and the school documents they couldn't repay; the school documents a likely inability to repay based on debt or income; or the parent lacks qualifying immigration status.

These do not qualify:

  • A parent who is eligible for PLUS but decides not to borrow.
  • A school that doesn't participate in PLUS, or an aid administrator's view that the parent shouldn't borrow.
  • A parent who has already borrowed the new $65,000 Parent PLUS maximum for you. The 2026 guidance says students aren't eligible for the extra unsubsidized amount in that case.

Before awarding the extra amount, the school is expected to check whether the parent could borrow with an endorser — someone without adverse credit who agrees to repay if the parent doesn't. And it must document the reason every year; a denial last year doesn't carry over automatically.

The 2026–27 part-time reduction

Starting with the 2026–27 award year, annual Direct Loan limits are reduced for students enrolled less than full time. Schools apply a schedule of reductions; James Madison University explains it as multiplying the full-time annual limit by your share of a full-time load. A third-year student with a PLUS denial taking 18 of 24 credits would see a $12,500 limit become about $9,375. Parent PLUS isn't subject to this reduction. Your school's exact calculation can differ by a few dollars from this estimate.

The new Parent PLUS limits

For loans made on or after July 1, 2026, Parent PLUS is capped at $20,000 a year and $65,000 in total per dependent student, counting all past borrowing for that student, including at other schools. Before, parents could borrow up to the full cost of attendance. The federal guidance addresses the $65,000 total; it doesn't say that reaching the $20,000 annual cap makes the student eligible for the extra unsubsidized amount, and a parent who borrowed $20,000 has obtained a PLUS Loan. Plan for the gap above $20,000 to come from savings, grants, a payment plan or a private loan, and ask your aid office before counting on anything else.

How to request it

  • Have the parent apply for the Parent PLUS Loan on StudentAid.gov so there is an official decision.
  • If denied, the parent can appeal with documentation, add an endorser, or stop there. The school will want to know which, because an approved appeal or endorser means the parent can borrow.
  • Submit your school's PLUS denial or additional unsubsidized loan request form. Most schools have one each award year.
  • Accept the increased Direct Unsubsidized amount in your student portal; first-time borrowers also need entrance counseling and a Master Promissory Note.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much more can I borrow if my parent is denied a Parent PLUS Loan? $4,000 more a year in your first and second years and $5,000 more from your third year on, all in Direct Unsubsidized Loans. That raises your annual limit to $9,500, $10,500 or $12,500 and your lifetime limit to $57,500.

Does my parent have to be denied, or can they just decline? They have to be unable to borrow. A parent who is eligible but chooses not to take a PLUS Loan doesn't make you eligible for the extra amount.

What if my parent can get PLUS with an endorser? Schools are expected to check whether the parent could borrow with an endorser before awarding the extra unsubsidized amount. If the parent can and does, you generally stay at the dependent limits.

Did the 2026 student loan changes affect this? The extra eligibility itself didn't change. What changed is the Parent PLUS cap — $20,000 a year and $65,000 total per student — and, starting in 2026–27, a reduction in annual limits for students enrolled less than full time.

If my parent hits the $65,000 Parent PLUS limit, can I borrow more? No. Federal guidance says students aren't eligible for the additional unsubsidized amount when their parents have reached the $65,000 aggregate limit.

Do I have to ask again every year? Yes. The school has to document that your parent still can't borrow each year, so expect to submit a new denial or request form.
