What the total includes

Application fees get most of the attention, but they're rarely the whole bill. This calculator adds the per-school application fee, a one-time standardized test fee, per-school score report fees (most tests include a handful of free reports at registration, with each additional one billed separately), per-school transcript fees, and any campus visit costs you enter — across every school on your list at once.

Ways to cut the total

  • Ask for application fee waivers. Many graduate programs waive the fee for applicants with financial need, for members of programs such as McNair Scholars, or for people who attend a recruitment event. Waivers often have to be requested before you submit, so check each program's admissions page early.
  • Use the GRE Fee Reduction Program. ETS cuts the registration fee to $100 for eligible applicants, including people who show financial need, receive unemployment benefits, or serve in the Peace Corps.
  • Send unofficial transcripts first. A lot of programs accept unofficial transcripts for review and only ask for official ones after admission, which saves the per-school transcript fee on schools that reject you.
  • Trim the list on fit, not fear. Each extra application adds a fee, a score report and a transcript. Two well-chosen schools you would actually attend cost less and help more than five safety applications.

Is this cost covered by financial aid?

Generally no. Application costs come before you enroll, so they are not part of any school's cost of attendance and federal student aid can't pay them. Budget for them from savings, and keep in mind that the real money question comes after admission: whether the program funds you through an assistantship or fellowship. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the average grad school application cost? Application fees alone typically run $60 to $100 per school, and most advisors recommend applying to four to six programs — so fees alone often total $300 to $600 before test fees, score reports, or transcripts. Professional programs often cost more per application.

Can I get application fees waived? Yes — most graduate programs offer fee waivers for applicants who demonstrate financial need, and some extend waivers to McNair Scholars or students who attend a recruiting event. Always ask the admissions office directly.

Do I need to pay a test fee for every school I apply to? No — you pay one fee to take the test itself, then a smaller fee for each additional official score report sent beyond the few included with registration.

Is it worth applying to more schools to increase my odds? There's a real tradeoff. Each additional school adds its own fees, so casting a wide net can add hundreds of dollars without proportionally increasing your chances if the extra schools aren't a good fit. A focused list of four to eight programs is usually more effective.
