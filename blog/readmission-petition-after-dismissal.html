How to Write a Readmission Petition After Academic Dismissal

7 min read · Updated October 2026

Academic dismissal is meant to be a serious consequence, but at many schools it is not the end of the road. Whether you can come back — and when — is set by your own school's catalog, and the rules differ more than most students expect. Two real policies show the range:

  • University of Tampa: a dismissed student may apply for readmission after one academic year (two regular semesters). The wait itself can be appealed to the Academic Appeals Committee — deadlines are June 1 for fall, January 2 for spring and April 1 for summer — with a written letter, supporting documents, and transcripts from any other school attended since dismissal.
  • Molloy University: a dismissed student "normally may not return to the University under any circumstances." A suspended student, by contrast, may return as a non-matriculated student with the Office of Student Success and Assessment's approval, and can apply for readmission after raising the cumulative GPA to 2.0.

How long you'll typically have to wait

Find the exact wait in your catalog's academic standing or dismissal section, and note whether the wait itself can be appealed (Tampa allows it). Also check whether your school distinguishes suspension from dismissal — at some schools "suspension" is the temporary status and "dismissal" the final one, and at others "dismissal" is the one-year separation. Your registrar or dean of students office can confirm which applies to you.

What a strong petition typically addresses

  • What caused the academic failure. Be specific and honest — vague references to "a hard time" tend to land weaker than a clear, concrete explanation.
  • What has concretely changed since then. This is often the most important part, and the easiest to get wrong by being too general.
  • What you'll do differently if readmitted. A specific plan — reduced course load, tutoring, a change in living situation, a different major — is generally viewed more favorably than a general promise to try harder.

Coursework elsewhere can help your case. Strong grades at another accredited institution are concrete evidence you can succeed now, and Tampa's appeal process explicitly asks for transcripts from schools attended since dismissal. Confirm in advance which credits, if any, would transfer back — don't assume.

If you're denied

Most schools allow you to petition again after a further waiting period, generally expecting new evidence beyond what you submitted the first time. Ask the review committee or dean's office directly what was missing from your petition — resubmitting essentially the same materials without addressing their specific concerns is unlikely to change the outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to wait before I can petition for readmission? It depends on your school. The University of Tampa allows readmission applications after one academic year (and lets students appeal the wait); Molloy University normally does not readmit dismissed students at all. Check your catalog's dismissal policy.

What should a readmission petition include? Most petitions should address what caused the failure, what has concretely changed, and what specific steps you'll take differently. Vague statements about trying harder are generally seen as weak.

Does taking classes elsewhere during dismissal help my petition? Often, yes — it is concrete evidence you can succeed now, and some schools (Tampa among them) ask for those transcripts in the appeal. Confirm in advance which credits might transfer back.

What if my readmission petition is denied? Most schools allow you to petition again after a further wait, generally expecting additional evidence. Ask what was missing before resubmitting.
