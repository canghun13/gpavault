The question that actually decides it

Every generic guide asks "what are your goals," but the more useful question is narrower: do you still want your original major, or not? If yes, you're choosing between a double major and a minor — both keep your original path intact, so the decision comes down to how much depth you want in the second field versus how much extra time and cost you're willing to take on. If no, none of that matters — a minor in your new interest doesn't solve the problem of a major you no longer want, and switching is the more honest answer even though it usually carries the largest cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a double major cost more than a major and a minor? Usually yes, since a double major requires completing full major-level coursework (typically 30-40+ credits) rather than the roughly 15-24 credits a minor requires. A double major is far less likely to fully overlap with your existing requirements.

Do employers actually care about a double major? It depends on the employer and role. For some technical or specialized positions a second major signals real depth, while for many other roles what you can demonstrably do matters far more than the credential on paper.

How do I know if switching majors entirely makes more sense than adding a minor? If you no longer want your original major at all, adding a minor doesn't solve that. Switching makes more sense when your interest in the original field has genuinely faded, not just when you've discovered a second interest alongside it.

Can I decide later, after starting one path? Often yes, especially early in college. Talk to your academic advisor about how late you can make this kind of change without losing progress.
