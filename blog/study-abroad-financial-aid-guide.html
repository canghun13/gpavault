Can You Use Financial Aid, Loans & Scholarships for Study Abroad?

8 min read · July 2026
Advertisement · Google AdSense

Cost is the single biggest reason students talk themselves out of studying abroad — but most students on financial aid can actually apply that same aid to a study abroad term. The catch is that it isn't automatic, and a few aid types genuinely don't transfer. Here's how to fund it without surprises.

What typically carries over

  • Federal grants and loans (Pell Grants, Direct Subsidized/Unsubsidized Loans) generally apply to study abroad programs affiliated with or approved by your school, up to your normal annual borrowing limits.
  • Institutional scholarships and grants from your home school usually apply, though some are restricted to specific terms or require full-time enrollment status abroad — confirm directly rather than assuming.
  • Private student loans can typically be used as well, though as with any private borrowing, compare rates and terms carefully against remaining federal options first.

What usually doesn't carry over

Federal Work-Study is the most common casualty, since it requires an actual job that isn't available while you're overseas. Some institution-specific scholarships are also restricted to on-campus enrollment only — read the fine print on any award tied to a specific campus location or research position.

The step people skip: notifying your financial aid office once you're accepted into a program. Your aid package is normally built around your home campus's standard cost of attendance — the office needs to manually adjust it to your study abroad program's actual budget, which can be higher or lower. Skipping this is the single most common cause of a funding shortfall discovered too late.

Dedicated study abroad scholarships

Beyond your regular aid package, look specifically for study abroad scholarships and grants — need-based options like the Gilman Scholarship for Pell-eligible students, destination-specific awards, and scholarships offered directly by program providers. These are frequently underused simply because students don't think to look beyond their standard financial aid office.

Home-school programs vs. third-party providers

Programs run directly by your school, or through a formal affiliate partnership, are almost always the easiest to fund with existing aid, since your school has already pre-approved them for aid application. Independent third-party programs may still qualify for federal aid in many cases, but confirm with your financial aid office before committing — some aid types genuinely won't apply to non-affiliated programs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my financial aid package automatically apply to a study abroad program? Not automatically — you generally need to notify your financial aid office once accepted, so they can adjust your aid package to the program's actual cost of attendance. Skipping this step is a common cause of funding surprises.

Can I use work-study earnings for study abroad? Generally no. Federal Work-Study requires an actual on-campus or approved local job, which isn't available while you're abroad.

Do scholarships specifically for study abroad exist? Yes — dedicated scholarships and grants exist at the federal, state, and program-provider level, including the Gilman Scholarship for Pell-eligible students. Check with your study abroad office.

Is it better to use a program run by my home school, or a third-party provider? Programs run directly by your home school or through a formal affiliate partnership are almost always easier to fund with existing aid. Confirm with your financial aid office before committing to an independent provider.
