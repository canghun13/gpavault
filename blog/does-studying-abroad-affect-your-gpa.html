Does Studying Abroad Affect Your GPA?

7 min read · July 2026
Advertisement · Google AdSense

Ask ten students this question and you'll likely get answers that flatly contradict each other — and they might all be right, because the answer is set entirely by each home institution's own policy, not by any national standard. Here's what the range actually looks like and how to find out where your school falls.

The three common policies

PolicyWhat happens to your GPAWhat still matters
Pass/Fail (CR/NC) onlyNo impact at all — credits count, grades don'tYou still must pass to earn credit; a Fail can hurt financial aid SAP pace
Full grade transferHost-institution grades are converted and fully averaged into your cumulative GPAGrading norms at your host school — harder or easier than home — directly change your GPA
Hybrid / program-dependentDepends on whether the program is run directly by your school or through a third-party affiliateAsk specifically about your chosen program, not just your school's general policy

Why this genuinely varies so much

Universities set their own policy because there's no consistent way to translate every host institution's grading system, and because study abroad offices differ in whether they treat the term as primarily an academic-record event or primarily an experiential one. Some schools that fully transfer grades do so because their exchange partners use comparable, well-understood grading scales (common with long-standing exchange partnerships), while schools that default to pass/fail often do so specifically to protect students from unfamiliar or harsher foreign grading curves.

The grading curve risk is real and easy to miss. If your program records actual letter grades into your GPA, ask your study abroad advisor specifically about grading norms at your host institution — some university systems abroad grade noticeably harder on average than typical US institutions, and your home school usually won't adjust for that difference.

How to find your school's actual policy

  • Check your study abroad office's website under "credit and grading" or similar — most publish this explicitly.
  • Ask specifically about your program type. A policy can differ between your school's own faculty-led programs, formal exchange partnerships, and third-party affiliate providers.
  • Don't assume based on a friend's experience at a different program or a different year — policies do get updated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my study abroad grades show up on my transcript? Almost always yes, in some form — but how they're recorded varies. Some schools list actual letter or numeric grades. Others convert everything to Credit/No Credit regardless of your actual grade. Check your specific study abroad office's policy before you go.

If my grades are Pass/Fail, is there any GPA risk at all? The main risk shifts from your GPA to your financial aid Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) status, which typically cares about completion rate rather than grade. A Fail can still hurt your SAP pace even if it never touches your GPA.

Do grad schools and employers see study abroad grades differently? Graduate and professional school applications often require an official transcript from the host institution too — meaning your actual host-school grades may be visible even if your home GPA shows Pass/Fail.

Does a harder grading curve abroad get adjusted for? Rarely. Grading norms vary significantly by country, and most home schools don't adjust incoming grades to compensate. Ask your study abroad advisor about grading norms at your specific host institution before you go.
