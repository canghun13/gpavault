Average Student Loan Debt by Major: Is Yours Normal? (2026)

8 min read · Updated October 2026

Knowing the typical student loan debt for your major is useful, but it doesn't answer the question that matters: whether your debt is manageable on the salary you are likely to earn. This guide gives sourced debt figures by major, shows what each balance costs per month, and explains why your school and your private loans move the number more than your major does.

Average student loan debt at graduation (2026)

According to the College Board's Trends in College Pricing and Student Aid 2025, 47% of 2023–24 bachelor's degree recipients at public and private nonprofit four-year colleges graduated with debt. Among those who borrowed, the average was $29,560. Federal loans averaged $20,460, and the 10% who also took private loans averaged $42,170 in private debt alone.

The surprise in the data: typical debt barely changes by major. Most common majors land between about $22,500 and $29,500, a gap of roughly $80 a month. What changes a lot by major is salary, which is why the same balance can be easy for one graduate and a strain for another.

Majors people ask about that aren't listed by name

  • Environmental science: not reported separately. Environmental engineering, the closest match, is $27,586.
  • Graphic design: reported as graphic communications, $29,383, the highest in the table above.
  • Forensic science: not listed. Criminal justice, where many forensic programs sit, is $25,527.
  • Animal care or pre-vet: animal sciences is $22,514. Veterinary school debt comes on top of this and is far larger.

Why "average" debt is misleading

Three things push a real balance far from the medians above:

  • Private loans. Federal loans for dependent undergraduates are capped at $31,000 in total, which is why federal medians cluster in the $20,000s. Students who fill the gap with private loans averaged another $42,170 in 2023–24, which can more than double the total.
  • School choice. Two students in the same major can graduate with very different balances depending on net price. Choosing a lower-cost school usually moves debt more than choosing a different major.
  • Time to degree. A fifth year adds another year of borrowing at the same annual limits.

Debt vs. salary: the check that matters

A common benchmark is to keep total student debt at or below your expected first-year salary, and to keep the monthly payment under about 10% of your gross income. For the median balances above, a payment of $256 to $334 a month stays under 10% of income at a salary of roughly $31,000 to $40,000. Most majors clear that. The risk comes from balances well above the medians, usually because of private loans or an expensive school.

Salaries differ more than debt does. NACE's Winter 2026 Salary Survey projects average starting salaries of $81,535 for computer science graduates and $66,155 for social sciences graduates, for example. Pre-med and pre-law: the debt layering problem

Students headed for medicine, law, dentistry or pharmacy carry undergraduate debt into a professional program that often costs far more. The undergraduate figures above are only the first layer. Since July 1, 2026, new professional students can borrow up to $50,000 a year and $200,000 in total in federal unsubsidized loans, and Grad PLUS is closed to new borrowers, so funding gaps beyond that come from private loans, scholarships or savings. What to do if your debt is already high

  • Income-driven repayment: plans such as IBR and the new RAP set payments by income rather than balance, which makes a large federal balance manageable on a modest salary. See repayment plans explained.
  • Public Service Loan Forgiveness: if you work for a government agency or qualifying nonprofit, PSLF forgives the remaining federal balance after 120 qualifying payments. For teachers, social workers and public health workers it is often the best path.
  • Private loans: these have no income-driven option. If your credit and income have improved since you borrowed, refinancing them can lower the rate. Refinancing federal loans into a private loan gives up federal protections, so keep the two decisions separate.
  • Pay extra early: extra payments in the first years after graduation reduce principal while interest is highest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average student loan debt for a bachelor's degree? Among 2023–24 bachelor's degree recipients at public and private nonprofit four-year colleges who borrowed, the average was $29,560, according to the College Board. About 47% of graduates had any debt. Federal loans averaged $20,460.

Which majors have the most student loan debt? In College Scorecard-based data compiled by the Education Data Initiative, most common bachelor's majors fall between about $22,500 and $29,500 in median federal debt. Graphic communications ($29,383) and education ($29,134) are near the top of that range, and computer science ($23,184) and animal sciences ($22,514) are near the bottom.

What is considered normal student loan debt? A balance near the medians above, roughly $22,500 to $29,500 in federal loans, is typical for a bachelor's degree. A more useful test is whether your total debt is at or below your expected first-year salary and your monthly payment stays under about 10% of gross income.

Do these figures include private loans? No. College Scorecard counts federal loans only. Graduates who also took private loans averaged $42,170 in private debt in 2023–24, so your total can be much higher than the median for your major.

Does graduate school debt get added to undergraduate debt? Yes. Undergraduate and graduate loans are separate debts that you repay together. Since July 1, 2026, new graduate students can borrow up to $100,000 and new professional students up to $200,000 in federal unsubsidized loans, and Grad PLUS is closed to new borrowers.

Can I reduce debt after I've already borrowed? For federal loans, income-driven repayment can lower monthly payments and PSLF can forgive the balance for qualifying public service work. Private loans can sometimes be refinanced at a lower rate. Extra payments early on reduce principal the fastest.
