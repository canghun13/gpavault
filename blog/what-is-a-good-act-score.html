What Is a Good ACT Score? 2026 Percentiles by College Tier

9 min read · Updated October 2026

Like the SAT, a "good" ACT score only makes sense relative to your target schools. A 26 is an excellent score for one university and a reach for another. This guide breaks down what counts as a good ACT composite by national percentile, by college selectivity tier, and by individual section, so you can judge your score against the right benchmark instead of a single national number.

The national average, and why it isn't the real benchmark

The average ACT composite for the class of 2025 was 19.4, among almost 1.4 million graduates who took the test, according to ACT. On ACT's national ranks (2022–2024 graduates), a 19 is the 55th percentile and a 20 the 61st — but most students researching "what is a good ACT score" are targeting schools well above the national average, where the relevant comparison is each school's middle 50% range.

Why your composite can hide your real strengths

A 26 composite could mean four evenly balanced section scores, or it could mean a 30 in English and Reading paired with a 22 in Math and Science. These two profiles look very different to admissions committees, especially at schools with strong STEM programs where Math and Science scores get extra informal weight, or liberal arts programs that lean toward English and Reading strength. Some schools also use section scores directly for course placement — a 28 in Math might place you into calculus regardless of your composite.

What each section tests, and where "good" differs

The ACT changed in 2025. On the enhanced ACT (online national tests from April 2025, paper national tests from September 2025, state and district testing from spring 2026), English has 50 questions in 35 minutes, Math 45 questions in 50 minutes with four answer choices, and Reading 36 questions in 40 minutes. Science (40 questions, 40 minutes) is now optional and no longer part of the composite, which is the average of English, Math and Reading; if you take Science, it is reported separately and feeds a STEM score.

A "good" section score is relative to your intended major the same way it is on the SAT: quantitative programs tend to look closely at Math (and Science, if you took it), even though the composite averages only English, Math and Reading.

ACT vs SAT: does it matter which one you submit?

Virtually all US colleges accept both tests equally, with no preference given to either. The ACT offers an optional Science section and, even after the 2025 changes, moves at a faster pace per question; the SAT leans more on extended reading passages and data interpretation without a separate Science section. Taking a full-length practice test of each is the most reliable way to determine which format suits you — many students score meaningfully better, in percentile terms, on one test than the other.

Test-optional: should you submit a "good enough" ACT score?

The same framework that applies to the SAT applies here: if your composite sits at or above a school's published 25th percentile, submitting generally strengthens your application. If you're meaningfully below that threshold, going test-optional at that specific school may be the stronger move, letting your GPA and coursework carry more weight. Between the 25th and 50th percentile, it's a genuine judgment call based on the strength of the rest of your file.

Scholarship thresholds worth knowing

Many merit scholarship programs set specific ACT cutoffs. Thresholds vary significantly by institution — always check each target school's specific scholarship page rather than assuming a national standard, since a 30 that qualifies for a large award at one school may fall just below the cutoff at another.

When retaking the ACT is worth it

Many schools that accept the ACT also superscore it, combining your best section scores across multiple sittings into a new composite. A retake is generally worth pursuing when your practice test scores under timed conditions run notably above your official composite, or when your weakest section has improved substantially since your last attempt. Most students see their biggest single jump between a first and second sitting — gains beyond a third attempt tend to be smaller without significant additional prep in between.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 24 a good ACT score? A 24 is the 79th percentile on ACT's national ranks, well above the class of 2025 average of 19.4, and solid for many state universities, though below the middle 50% range at selective schools such as Ohio State (29–33).

Is a 30 ACT score competitive for selective schools? Yes. A 30 is the 94th percentile nationally and falls inside Ohio State's 29–33 admitted range, though below the ranges at the most selective schools, such as MIT's 34–36.

What ACT score do you need for the Ivy League? The most selective colleges admit mostly students at the 98th–99th percentile or above; MIT's class of 2029 middle 50% was 34–36. Scores below that can still be competitive with an otherwise exceptional application.

Is the ACT easier than the SAT? Neither test is inherently easier — they emphasize different skills (faster pace and an optional Science section on the ACT; more extended reading and data analysis on the SAT). Individual students often perform better on one than the other, which is why taking a practice test of each is worth doing before committing.
