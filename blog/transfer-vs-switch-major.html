Transfer Schools vs. Switch Majors: Which Costs You Less?

7 min read · July 2026
Advertisement · Google AdSense

Roughly a third of undergraduates change their major at least once, and a similar share of students transfer schools at some point. When both are on the table at the same time — you're unhappy with your major AND your school — most advice just tells you to "consider your goals." Here's the actual cost comparison.

Why switching majors is usually the cheaper move

When you switch majors at your school you're already attending, your general education credits and most electives carry over automatically — you never left, so nothing needs to be re-evaluated by a new registrar. Only your major-specific coursework is at risk of not counting toward the new major. Transferring puts your entire transcript through a new school's evaluation, general education included, and federal data has found that transfer students lose an average of around 13 credits — roughly a full semester's worth — with much larger losses common in some school pairings.

When transfer makes sense despite the cost: if your current school simply doesn't offer the major you want, or the program you want is meaningfully stronger elsewhere (better placement outcomes, research opportunities, faculty), the credit-loss cost can still be worth paying. The comparison above is about cost, not about which option is "right" for every situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it always cheaper to switch majors at my current school than to transfer? Not always, but usually, because transferring risks losing credits across your entire transcript while switching majors typically only affects major-specific credits. Run both scenarios through the calculators to check your specific case.

What if my current school doesn't offer the major I want? Transferring may be your only realistic option regardless of cost. Use the Transfer Credit Loss Calculator to compare potential destination schools on how many credits they'd preserve.

Does transferring always take longer to finish a degree? Not necessarily, especially for well-planned community-college-to-university transfers using a formal articulation agreement. It's transfers without a strong credit-transfer relationship where delays are common.

Can I do both at once — transfer and change majors? Yes, but it typically compounds the credit loss of both moves. Doing one change at a time often preserves more credits than doing both together.
