What Is the Dean's List GPA Requirement? 2026 Guide

10 min read · Updated October 2026

The Dean's List recognizes students who earn high grades in a single term. Unlike Latin honors, which are based on your full record at graduation, the Dean's List is awarded term by term, so you can earn it some semesters and not others.

There is no national standard; each school sets its own GPA cutoff, credit minimum and exclusions. Below are the actual rules at nine schools that publish them, what they have in common, and the details that disqualify students with a qualifying GPA.

GPAs are for that semester unless noted, and hours are graded (letter-grade) credit hours that term. What the table shows:

  • 3.5 is the usual floor. Every school here starts its Dean's List or equivalent honor at 3.5. Texas A&M reserves the name "Dean's Honor Roll" for 3.75 and up, and calls 3.50–3.74 the Distinguished Student list.
  • The credit minimum is 12 to 15 graded hours. Texas A&M and Arizona require 15, which is more than a 12-hour full-time load. Arizona gives 12-unit students with a 3.5 an "Honorable Mention" instead.
  • "Graded" is the key word. Pass/fail, audit and similar credits generally do not count toward the minimum, even though they do not lower your GPA.

Semester GPA or cumulative GPA?

At eight of the nine schools above, only that term's GPA counts, so a student with a 3.1 cumulative GPA makes the list with a 3.6 semester, and a student with a 3.6 cumulative misses it with a 3.4 semester. Purdue is the exception: its Dean's List requires a 3.5 cumulative GPA plus at least a 3.0 that semester, and it uses a separate "Semester Honors" for a 3.5 term GPA. If your school's catalog does not say "semester," check before assuming.

That is also the answer to "is a 3.39 Dean's List?" At every school in the table, no: 3.39 is below the 3.5 floor whether it is your semester or cumulative GPA. And honors in one semester do not carry over; each term is judged separately.

What disqualifies you even with a qualifying GPA

  • Too few graded hours. A 4.0 on 9 credits misses a 12-hour rule. Ohio University and Ohio State have separate lists for part-time students: Ohio University's Provost's List takes a 3.5 on 6 to 11.99 graded hours, and Ohio State's part-time Dean's List looks at 12 or more graded hours across three terms in which you were never full-time.
  • Incompletes and missing grades. Ohio State bars students with any unresolved I or missing mark, Arizona requires incompletes to be made up first, and DePaul calculates the term GPA only after IN, M or R grades are resolved.
  • Failing or no-pass marks. Ohio State excludes anyone with an E, NP or U that term, regardless of GPA in other courses.
  • Pass/fail credits. Missouri State and Arizona exclude pass/fail and audited credit from the hours count. Ohio State allows up to 3 S/U hours, but only if you earn an S.
  • Grade forgiveness after the fact. At Ohio State, retaking a course later never adds Dean's List status to the earlier term.

Dean's List vs. President's List and other tiers

Many schools split high-GPA terms into tiers, and the names vary:

  • Ohio University: President's List for a 4.0 with 12 graded hours, Dean's List for 3.5–3.999, Provost's List for part-time students.
  • Texas A&M: Dean's Honor Roll at 3.75–4.00 and Distinguished Student at 3.50–3.74, both with 15 graded hours.
  • University of Arizona: Dean's List with Distinction for a 4.000, Dean's List for 3.500–3.999, Honorable Mention for 12-unit students with 3.500 or above.
  • Purdue: Dean's List (cumulative-based) and Semester Honors (term-based) are two separate honors.

"Honor roll" and "Chancellor's List" have no fixed meaning; check your own catalog for the names and cutoffs. For how term honors relate to graduation honors such as cum laude, see Dean's List vs. Latin Honors.

"Dean's Lister" and GWA outside the US

In the Philippines, "Dean's Lister" is the usual term, and schools use a General Weighted Average (GWA) or GPA on a 1.00–5.00 scale where lower is better: 1.00 is the top grade and 5.00 is failing. A cutoff of 1.75 is common. At Bukidnon State University, for example, a student needs 1.75 or better with no grade lower than 2.00 and a load of at least 18 units, plus no late drops or excess absences. Some universities use other titles, such as "University Scholar," with their own cutoffs, so confirm the rule with your registrar.

Does Dean's List matter beyond recognition?

It is a standard line in the Education or Honors section of a resume, especially for internships and first jobs, and it shows a pattern of strong terms on graduate school applications. Its weight fades after the first few years of work, as GPA does. It usually does not trigger money by itself: financial aid renewal depends on cumulative GPA and Satisfactory Academic Progress, and merit scholarships are typically awarded at admission with their own renewal GPA. Frequently asked questions

What GPA do you need for the Dean's List? Usually a 3.5 semester GPA. All nine schools in our table start their Dean's List or equivalent honor at 3.5, though Texas A&M uses 3.75 for its "Dean's Honor Roll" and 3.50–3.74 for a Distinguished Student list. You also need 12 to 15 graded credit hours that term.

Is Dean's List based on cumulative GPA or semester GPA? Semester GPA at most schools, including Ohio State, Penn State, Missouri State and Texas A&M. Purdue is a notable exception: its Dean's List requires a 3.5 cumulative GPA plus a 3.0 that semester. Each term is judged on its own, so nothing carries over automatically.

Is a 3.39 GPA Dean's List? Not at any of the schools in our table, all of which start at 3.5. A 3.39 can still qualify for a lower tier at schools that have one, so check your catalog.

How do you get on the Dean's List? Earn a semester GPA at or above your school's cutoff while taking at least the minimum graded load (12 hours at Ohio State, Missouri State and DePaul; 15 at Texas A&M and Arizona), with no incompletes or failing marks that term. Recognition is automatic from final grades; there is no application.

Can part-time students make the Dean's List? Usually not on the main list, because of the 12-hour minimum, but some schools have a part-time version. Ohio University's Provost's List takes a 3.5 on 6 to 11.99 graded hours, and Ohio State's part-time Dean's List uses 12 graded hours across three part-time terms.

How many students can be on the Dean's List? At schools with a fixed GPA cutoff, like every school in our table, there is no cap: everyone who meets the GPA and credit rules is listed. No national figure exists for the share of students who make it.

Does Dean's List affect financial aid? Not directly. Aid renewal depends on cumulative GPA and Satisfactory Academic Progress, and most merit scholarships are awarded at admission with their own renewal rules, so a 4.0 Dean's List semester does not by itself unlock new merit aid. Some departmental scholarships do list academic honors as a criterion.

Can you lose Dean's List status after earning it? Normally no; it is recorded from a completed term's final grades and, at schools like Arizona, appears on the transcript. A later grade change from an academic integrity finding can remove it.

What is a Dean's Lister, and how is GWA different from GPA? "Dean's Lister" is the Philippine term for a Dean's List honoree. GWA runs from 1.00 to 5.00 with lower being better. A 1.75 cutoff is common; Bukidnon State requires 1.75 or better with no grade below 2.00 and at least 18 units.
