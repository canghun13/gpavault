FAFSA ID Verification: What Changed in 2026

6 min read · Updated October 2026

Since April 26, 2026, the FAFSA process includes a new step that catches a lot of applicants off guard: a real-time identity check built directly into the form. Here's what it is, who it affects, and how it differs from the older financial-data verification process most FAFSA guides already cover.

What the new requirement actually is

On Sunday, April 26, 2026, Federal Student Aid added real-time identity fraud detection to the FAFSA. Applicants the system flags as high-risk must hold one government-issued ID up to the camera for a photo and then complete a brief live camera check. Accepted IDs are a driver's license, passport, tribal identification card or permanent resident card. The check runs on a phone or tablet; if you start on a computer, the form shows a QR code to continue on a mobile device, and the session has to be finished in one sitting. The Department also ran a one-time fraud screen of 2026–27 FAFSAs submitted before the launch, so some students who had already filed were flagged afterward without doing anything new.

Why this change happened

The Department of Education has framed the new identity check as a fraud-prevention measure, aimed at stopping bad actors from submitting FAFSA applications using stolen or fabricated identities to redirect financial aid disbursements. Identity theft targeting the financial aid system isn't new, but requiring a real-time photo — rather than accepting an uploaded image — closes a specific gap that static document uploads couldn't: it makes it much harder to submit someone else's photo ID, or an old or edited image, in place of a live capture taken at the moment of application.

Tips for a smooth verification

  • Have your physical ID ready before you start the FAFSA — a driver's license, passport, tribal ID or permanent resident card, not a photo of one saved on your phone.
  • Make sure you're in a well-lit space with your camera working properly, since a blurry or poorly lit capture can require you to retry the process.
  • Keep a phone or tablet handy — the ID check runs on a mobile device, and starting on a computer gives you a QR code to switch over. The check can't be paused and resumed.
  • If you're flagged after already submitting, check your FAFSA Submission Summary and your college portal regularly, since the retroactive screening may not always trigger an obvious notification right away.

This is not the same as financial data verification

FAFSA has long used a separate process — often just called "verification" — where a share of applicants are asked to confirm the accuracy of income and household information reported on their form, usually initiated by their college's financial aid office. ID verification is different: it confirms who you are, not the accuracy of your financial data, and it can happen during the FAFSA form itself rather than being assigned afterward.

If you can't complete the photo step, you're not stuck. You can still submit the FAFSA, but it is processed with a reject code (Reject Code 74), and you must contact your college's financial aid office, which completes identity verification before aid can be disbursed. Budget extra time for this if it applies to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the new FAFSA ID verification requirement? Since April 26, 2026, the FAFSA includes real-time fraud screening. Applicants flagged as high-risk must photograph a government-issued ID on a phone or tablet and complete a live camera check. It is separate from the longstanding financial-data verification process.

What happens if I can't complete the photo ID step? You can still submit the FAFSA, but it will carry a reject code until you contact your college's financial aid office and complete identity verification there. Aid can't be disbursed until that is resolved.

Is ID verification the same thing as being selected for financial data verification? No — these are separate processes. Financial data verification confirms your income/household information; ID verification confirms your identity to prevent fraud.

Does this affect students with an unaccompanied homeless youth determination? No exemption — a student could be selected for ID verification regardless of homeless youth determination status, since they're separate processes.
