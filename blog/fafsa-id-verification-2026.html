FAFSA ID Verification: What Changed in 2026

6 min read · July 2026
Advertisement · Google AdSense

Since late April 2026, the FAFSA process includes a new step that catches a lot of applicants off guard: a real-time identity check built directly into the form. Here's what it is, who it affects, and how it differs from the older financial-data verification process most FAFSA guides already cover.

What the new requirement actually is

Beginning around April 26, 2026, Federal Student Aid started screening all FAFSA applicants for identity verification. Some students are prompted to take a real-time photo of a government-issued ID — a driver's license or state ID — directly within the FAFSA form. Notably, you can't upload an existing photo from your device; the system requires a live capture, specifically to prevent fraud using stolen or reused images. The Department has also screened previously submitted 2026-27 FAFSAs retroactively, meaning some students who already filed could be assigned a new identity verification requirement after the fact.

Why this change happened

The Department of Education has framed the new identity check as a fraud-prevention measure, aimed at stopping bad actors from submitting FAFSA applications using stolen or fabricated identities to redirect financial aid disbursements. Identity theft targeting the financial aid system isn't new, but requiring a real-time photo — rather than accepting an uploaded image — closes a specific gap that static document uploads couldn't: it makes it much harder to submit someone else's photo ID, or an old or edited image, in place of a live capture taken at the moment of application.

Tips for a smooth verification

  • Have your physical ID ready before you start the FAFSA — a driver's license or state ID, not a photo of one saved on your phone.
  • Make sure you're in a well-lit space with your camera working properly, since a blurry or poorly lit capture can require you to retry the process.
  • Complete the FAFSA on a device with a working camera — if you're using a shared or older computer without one, plan to switch to a phone or another device for this step.
  • If you're flagged after already submitting, check your FAFSA Submission Summary and your college portal regularly, since the retroactive screening may not always trigger an obvious notification right away.

This is not the same as financial data verification

FAFSA has long used a separate process — often just called "verification" — where roughly a third of applicants are asked to confirm the accuracy of income and household information reported on their form, usually initiated by their college's financial aid office. ID verification is different: it confirms who you are, not the accuracy of your financial data, and it can happen during the FAFSA form itself rather than being assigned afterward.

If you can't complete the photo step, you're not stuck. Students unable to complete real-time ID verification during the FAFSA can still submit the form — but they'll need to provide a government-issued ID to their college's financial aid office before aid can be disbursed. Budget extra time for this if it applies to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the new FAFSA ID verification requirement? Starting late April 2026, Federal Student Aid began screening all applicants to verify identity, with some asked to take a real-time photo of a government ID during the FAFSA form itself. This is separate from the longstanding financial-data verification process.

What happens if I can't complete the photo ID step? You can still submit the FAFSA, but you'll need to provide a government-issued ID to your college's financial aid office before aid can be disbursed.

Is ID verification the same thing as being selected for financial data verification? No — these are separate processes. Financial data verification confirms your income/household information; ID verification confirms your identity to prevent fraud.

Does this affect students with an unaccompanied homeless youth determination? No exemption — a student could be selected for ID verification regardless of homeless youth determination status, since they're separate processes.
